What is Descriptive Text?

Descriptive Text is a text that describes a particular person, place, or thing in detail.

When we describe a person, we tell the reader about their physical appearance (what they look like) and their personality traits (how they act/behave).

What is the Social Function / Purpose?

📌 To describe and reveal a specific person in detail.

The goal is to give a vivid picture of a specific person so that the reader can picture them clearly in their mind!

Generic Structure of Descriptive Text

A descriptive text consists of two main sections:

Part 1

1. Identification

Located in the first paragraph. It introduces the specific person who is going to be described (e.g., name, relationship, general info).

Part 2

2. Description

Located in the subsequent paragraphs. It gives specific details about the person, such as physical appearance, clothes, hobbies, habits, and personality.

Sample Text: "My English Teacher, Mr. Alex" Click highlighted parts!
[ IDENTIFICATION ] My favorite English teacher is Mr. Alex. He is thirty years old and teaches grade seven at my school. Everyone loves him because he is very friendly.
[ DESCRIPTION ] Mr. Alex is tall and slim. He has short black hair and friendly brown eyes. He usually wears a neat shirt and glasses. He is very patient and kind to all students. In his free time, he likes playing guitar and reading novels.

Language Features: Useful Adjectives

When describing people, we use Adjectives (Describing Words). Here is your vocabulary toolkit for 7th Grade!

Physical Appearance (Looks)

Height & Body: Tall, short, slim, chubby, athletic, thin.
Hair: Long, short, curly, straight, wavy, dark, blonde.
Face & Eyes: Round, oval, big eyes, sharp nose, bright smile.
Age & Style: Young, elderly, trendy, neat, handsome, pretty.

Personality Traits (Character)

Kind / Friendly: Warm, helpful, caring.
Funny / Cheerful: Makes people laugh, joyful.
Smart / Clever: Quick learner, intelligent.
Diligent / Hardworking: Works hard, disciplined.
Patient: Calm, doesn't get angry easily.
Polite: Well-mannered, respectful.
Key Grammar

Simple Present Tense

We use the Simple Present Tense in descriptive texts because we describe facts, general truths, and regular habits of a person.

💡 Verbal Sentences use main verbs (actions/habits) like speak, live, wear, play, like, swim.
Sentence Type Formula / Pattern Examples
+ Positive (+) S + V1 (s/es) + Object
• She wears glasses every day.
• They play football together.
- Negative (-) S + do / does + NOT + V1 + Object
• She does not (doesn't) wear glasses.
• They do not (don't) play football.
? Interrogative (?) Do / Does + S + V1 + Object?
• Does she wear glasses?
• Do they play football?
⚠️ Verb S/ES Rules:

For singular subjects (He, She, It, Mr. Alex), add -s / -es to verb in positive sentence (e.g., he plays, she teaches).

For subjects (I, You, We, They), use base verb (e.g., they play, we teach).

💡 Nominal Sentences do NOT have action verbs. They use To Be (am / is / are) followed by Adjectives, Nouns, or Adverbs.
Sentence Type Formula / Pattern Examples
+ Positive (+) S + is / am / are + Complement (Adj/Noun)
• He is very friendly.
• They are 7th grade students.
• I am tall and energetic.
- Negative (-) S + is / am / are + NOT + Complement
• He is not (isn't) friendly.
• They are not (aren't) lazy.
? Interrogative (?) Is / Am / Are + S + Complement?
• Is he friendly?
• Are they students?
Subject "I" ➔ AM
He / She / It / Single Name ➔ IS
You / We / They / Plural ➔ ARE
